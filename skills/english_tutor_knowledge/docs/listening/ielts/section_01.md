# 城市化对生物多样性的影响

## 原文
Welcome to today's lecture on urban ecology. I'm Dr. Harrison from the Department of Environmental Studies, and I'll be examining the complex relationship between urbanization and biodiversity loss.

To begin with, let's establish some fundamental concepts. Biodiversity, as most of you know, encompasses the variety of life at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels. Urbanization, meanwhile, refers to the process of population concentration in cities and the accompanying transformation of land use. The intersection of these phenomena presents one of the most pressing environmental challenges of our time.

Research conducted over the past two decades has consistently demonstrated that urban expansion correlates strongly with habitat fragmentation. When natural landscapes are converted into built environments, continuous ecosystems become isolated patches. This fragmentation impedes species movement, disrupts migration patterns, and ultimately reduces genetic diversity within populations.

Consider, for example, the case of the Florida panther. As urban development encroached upon the Everglades, these large mammals found their habitat increasingly bisected by roads and residential areas. The resulting genetic isolation led to inbreeding depression, necessitating controversial intervention programs to introduce genetic diversity from Texas cougars.

However, the relationship between cities and biodiversity is not exclusively antagonistic. Recent scholarship has highlighted the concept of 'urban adapters'—species that not only tolerate but thrive in human-modified environments. Peregrine falcons, once endangered due to pesticide use, have made remarkable recoveries by nesting on skyscrapers that simulate their natural cliff habitats. Similarly, coyotes have expanded their range dramatically across North America, exploiting urban green spaces and anthropogenic food sources.

This leads us to an important theoretical framework: the intermediate disturbance hypothesis. Originally developed to explain species diversity in ecological communities, this hypothesis suggests that moderate levels of disturbance can actually promote biodiversity by preventing competitive exclusion. Applied to urban contexts, this suggests that cities with heterogeneous landscapes—parks, gardens, green roofs, and restored wetlands—may support surprisingly rich biological communities.

The implications for conservation policy are substantial. Traditional approaches have emphasized preserving pristine wilderness areas, often neglecting urban ecosystems. Yet with projections indicating that nearly 70% of the global population will reside in cities by 2050, integrating biodiversity considerations into urban planning becomes imperative.

Singapore provides an exemplary model. Through its 'City in a Garden' initiative, the nation-state has implemented mandatory green building standards, created extensive park connector networks, and established rigorous protections for remnant forest patches. These measures have enabled the persistence of numerous native species, including the critically endangered Singapore freshwater crab.

In conclusion, while urbanization undoubtedly poses significant threats to global biodiversity, thoughtful design and management of urban spaces can mitigate these impacts. The challenge for the next generation of environmental professionals will be to reconcile the seemingly contradictory demands of urban development and ecological preservation.

Thank you for your attention. I'll now take questions.

## 翻译
欢迎来到今天的城市生态学讲座。我是环境研究系的哈里森博士，我将探讨城市化与生物多样性丧失之间的复杂关系。

首先，让我们建立一些基本概念。正如你们大多数人所知道的，生物多样性包括基因、物种和生态系统层面的生命多样性。与此同时，城市化指的是人口向城市集中的过程以及随之而来的土地利用转变。这些现象的交汇构成了我们这个时代最紧迫的环境挑战之一。

过去二十年进行的研究一致表明，城市扩张与栖息地碎片化密切相关。当自然景观被转化为建筑环境时，连续的生态系统变成孤立的斑块。这种碎片化阻碍了物种移动，扰乱了迁徙模式，并最终减少了种群内的遗传多样性。

例如，考虑一下佛罗里达美洲豹的案例。随着城市发展侵入大沼泽地，这些大型哺乳动物发现它们的栖息地被道路和住宅区越来越多地分割。由此产生的遗传隔离导致了近亲繁殖衰退， necessitating 有争议的干预项目，从德克萨斯美洲狮引入遗传多样性。

然而，城市与生物多样性之间的关系并非完全对立。最近的学术研究强调了"城市适应者"的概念——这些物种不仅容忍而且在人类改造的环境中茁壮成长。游隼曾因杀虫剂使用而濒临灭绝，但通过在高楼大厦上筑巢（这些高楼模拟了它们自然的悬崖栖息地），它们已经显著恢复。同样，郊狼在北美大幅扩展了它们的分布范围，利用城市绿地和人为食物来源。

这引出了一个重要的理论框架：中度干扰假说。这个假说最初是为了解释生态群落中的物种多样性而发展起来的，它表明适度的干扰水平实际上可以通过防止竞争排斥来促进生物多样性。应用于城市环境，这表明具有异质景观的城市——公园、花园、绿色屋顶和恢复的湿地——可能支持 surprisingly 丰富的生物群落。

对保护政策的影响是重大的。传统方法强调保护原始荒野地区，往往忽视城市生态系统。然而，预测显示到2050年近70%的全球人口将居住在城市，将生物多样性考虑因素纳入城市规划变得势在必行。

新加坡提供了一个典范模式。通过其"花园中的城市"倡议，这个城邦国家实施了强制性的绿色建筑标准，创建了广泛的公园连接网络，并对残余森林斑块建立了严格的保护。这些措施使众多本地物种得以持续生存，包括极度濒危的新加坡淡水蟹。

总之，虽然城市化无疑对全球生物多样性构成重大威胁，但城市空间的周到设计和管理可以减轻这些影响。下一代环境专业人士面临的挑战将是调和城市发展与生态保护之间看似矛盾的需求。

谢谢你们的聆听。我现在接受提问。
